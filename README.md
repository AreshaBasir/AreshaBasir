## Current Research

### Physics-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Non-Myopic Active Navigation

**Undergraduate Thesis — University of Dhaka**

My current research investigates how a robot can perform **non-myopic information acquisition under realistic movement costs**.

Traditional exploration strategies often prioritize immediate information gain. However, acquiring information requires the robot to physically move through the environment, making the true decision problem a trade-off between **information value and acquisition cost**.

The research explores a framework combining:

**POMDPs → Semantic Scene Graphs → Information Gain → Physics-Aware Cost Modeling → Reinforcement Learning**

Key components include:

* PPO-based reinforcement learning
* Semantic Scene Graphs for scene-level reasoning
* Physics-Informed Neural Networks for movement-cost estimation
* Information gain modeling
* Physical movement and energy costs
* Non-myopic exploration
* Information Gain per Unit Cost

The broader objective is to move from:

> maximizing immediate information gain

toward:

> selecting observations according to their expected information value relative to the physical cost of acquiring them.

## Research Philosophy

I am interested in robotic systems where **perception, learning, planning, and physical interaction are treated as parts of a unified decision-making problem**.

Rather than asking only what a robot *can* perceive or where it *can* move, I am interested in systems that can reason about:

* What is currently known?
* What remains uncertain?
* What information is worth acquiring?
* What will it cost to acquire that information?
* How will that information affect the eventual decision?
